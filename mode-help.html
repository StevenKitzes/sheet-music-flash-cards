How it works

Who is this for?

Anyone learning to read the treble clef can use these flash cards, but a few features are made especially for guitarists.

  • For any musician: Guess Mode and Find Mode, choosing which notes to practice (above, on, or below the staff), practicing in any key and with sharps, flats and naturals, card and review timing, and keeping your screen awake while you study.
  • For guitarists: the string and fret list shown when an answer is revealed, and the "Max fret" and "Show frets" settings. Narrowing "Show frets" also limits which notes come up. If you don't play guitar, uncheck "Show" next to "Guitar frets" in Settings: the string and fret list goes away, the fret settings are ignored, and every note can come up, including ones a guitar can't play.
  • A note on octave numbers: this app uses guitar convention, where music is written one octave higher than it sounds. So the bottom line of the staff is called E3 here, while a pianist would call that same line E4. The letter names are the same for everyone; only the octave number is shifted.

Modes!

Guess Mode

In Guess Mode, you will be shown a note. All you have to do is determine what that note is. After a few seconds, you'll see the name of the note so you can check if you were right, along with every string and fret where that note can be played in standard tuning (up to the max fret you choose in Settings). To focus on one position on the neck, narrow the "Show frets" range in Settings (e.g. 5 to 9): only notes you can play within that range will come up (in both modes), and only positions within that range will be listed. Then a new note will be shown.

Find Mode

In Find Mode, you will be shown the full name of a note (including octave). You'll have some time to pick the correct location of that note on the staff/ledger lines. Press on the staff and a faint note appears where you're pointing; slide up or down to adjust it, then let go to lock in your answer. Slide off the staff before letting go to cancel.

  • As soon as you pick, the card skips straight to the answer. You get one pick per card.
  • If you pick the right note (in any octave), the screen will flash green! If you pick the wrong note, or run out of time, the screen will flash red.
  • Either way, the note you were looking for is shown as a filled note, and the same note in every other octave is shown as a hollow note.

Keys and accidentals

By default you practice in the key of C with no accidentals. In Settings you can change both:

  • Key: pick any of the 15 standard keys. Its key signature is drawn at the start of the staff, and every note follows it unless the note has its own accidental. For example, in G major a plain F on the staff is F♯.
  • Note accidentals: turn on Sharp (♯), Flat (♭) and/or Natural (♮) to have them show up on individual notes. Each card has an even chance of being a plain note or one of the accidentals you turned on. A note's accidental overrides the key signature, so a natural on F in G major makes it plain F. Naturals only appear in keys other than C, on notes the key signature changes.
  • You'll sometimes see less common spellings like E♯, B♯, C♭ and F♭. The answer tells you which plain note they sound the same as (E♯ is the same as F).
  • In Find Mode, an accidental never changes which line or space a note sits on, so you answer the same way: F♯4 goes on the F4 line. The string and fret list does include the accidental.

Settings (⚙︎)

Choose which notes to include (ledger notes above the staff, notes on the staff, ledger notes below), the key and note accidentals, how long each card is shown and how long the answer stays up, the guitar fret range, and whether to keep your screen awake while you study. Cards pause while Settings is open, and your choices are remembered on this device. When you leave out the upper or lower ledger notes, the staff zooms in on the notes you're practicing.
